The terms

Open any one. Each gives the plain answer, three things worth knowing, and how the claim is filed.

existential risk With AI, extinction is not the worst case. read

The permanent destruction of humanity’s long term potential.

Not the ending. The losing of every other ending.

  • Extinction. Nobody left. This is the one everybody pictures.
  • Permanent dystopia. Everyone alive. No way back. Forever is doing the work in that sentence.
  • Permanent stagnation. Nothing ends. Nothing improves. Ever.

People picture extinction, decide it sounds like science fiction, and dismiss the whole category. The claim being made is broader, and more plausible, than the one being dismissed.

definition Bostrom, Existential Risk Prevention as Global
Priority, Global Policy 4(1), 2013
misuse and misalignment AI misalignment needs no villain. read

The system pursues something other than what was intended.

No villain. No malfunction. The system works exactly as built and the outcome is still bad.

  • Misuse. A person deliberately uses a capable system to do damage.
  • Misalignment. The system pursues something other than what was meant.
  • Structural. Nobody misused it, nothing malfunctioned, and it still went badly.

Every plan that starts with “we just stop bad people using it” is aimed at one third of the problem. The other two thirds need something other than a rule about who is allowed to press the button.

definition Zwetsloot and Dafoe, Accidents, Misuse
and Structure, Lawfare, 11 February 2019
recursive self improvement AI that improves AI could be the last thing we invent. read

A hypothesis. A system good at AI research improves itself, and each version is better at improving.

Good wrote that down in 1965. Sixty years later it is still the argument everything else hangs on.

  • The loop must close. The system has to actually improve its own successor.
  • Gains must compound. Each round has to yield more than the last, not less.
  • Nothing must bottleneck. Not compute, not data, not physics, not money.

This is the one where being right too late is indistinguishable from being wrong. Which is why people who disagree about almost everything else still argue about this one carefully.

argument I. J. Good, Speculations Concerning the First
Ultraintelligent Machine, 1965; Carlsmith, 2022
