context window AI does not remember you. read

How much text it can hold in front of it at once.

Everything outside that window is gone. Not forgotten. Never held.

  • One conversation. Scroll far enough and the beginning falls out the back.
  • Nothing personal. Between chats it retains nothing about you at all.
  • Unless a product stores it. Any memory you experience was built around the model, not by it.

People tell these systems things they would not tell a search engine, on the assumption that something is keeping track. Something might be. It just is not the model. Worth knowing which product does what.

RLHF Nobody taught AI human values. read

A scorer, built from people comparing pairs of answers.

People compare. Those comparisons train a second model. That second model trains the first.

  • People compare. Which of these two answers is better. Nothing grander than that.
  • A scorer learns. A second model learns to predict those preferences.
  • The first model optimises. Against the scorer. Not against people, and not against truth.

If you optimise hard against a proxy, you get whatever scores well, which is not always what was wanted. This is why models can be agreeable rather than correct. Agreeable rates well.

measured Ouyang et al., InstructGPT, 2022;
Bai et al., Constitutional AI, Anthropic, 2022
open weights Once an AI model is released, nobody can take it back. read

The weights are published. Usually nothing else is.

You can download and run it. You still cannot see what it was trained on.

  • It cannot be recalled. Once the file is out, every copy is out. There is no undo.
  • Safeguards come off. Refusal behaviour can be removed cheaply by anyone who has it.
  • Not the same as open. Training data and code usually stay private. The name oversells it.

Open weights give researchers real access and break up concentration. They also hand capability to everyone at once. Both of those are true at the same time, and that is the whole difficulty.

compute Whether an AI is legally dangerous comes down to one count of sums. read

Floating point operations. How much arithmetic the training run did.

An odd thing to write into law, and currently the best proxy anyone has.

  • Ten to the 25. Above this, Europe presumes a model carries systemic risk.
  • Ten to the 26. Above this, California calls it a frontier model.
  • Twelve developers. Had trained above the European line, as of June 2025.

Compute is easy to count and only loosely related to whether a model is actually dangerous. It was chosen because it is measurable and hard to hide, not because it is the right thing to measure.

measured EU AI Act Article 51; California SB 53;
Epoch AI, models above 10^25 FLOP, June 2025
red teaming There are people whose entire job is making AI misbehave. read

Attacking your own system on purpose, before anybody else does.

The closest thing the field has to a fire drill, and it is still done by hand.

  • Thousands of attempts. One published Anthropic study logged 38,961 attacks on its own models.
  • Scale alone did not help. Only models trained on human feedback got harder to attack as they grew.
  • Still not solved. The UK AI Security Institute reports universal jailbreaks for every system it has tested.

Finding a flaw proves the flaw is there. Not finding one proves very little. This is also how the alarming headline experiments get made. Somebody was paid to make the model do the frightening thing.

measured Ganguli et al., Red Teaming Language Models,
Anthropic, 2022; UK AI Security Institute
intelligence Nobody can define intelligence, in AI or in us. read

It scores well on tests. That is nearly always the whole claim.

Measurable, narrow, and much smaller than the word suggests.

  • Seventy definitions. Legg and Hutter collected around seventy in 2007.
  • No convergence. The field has not agreed on one in the years since.
  • So tests stand in. And test scores drift for reasons other than capability.

A claim about a benchmark score gets restated as a claim about a mind, usually within one sentence. Both sides do it. Boosters slide up, sceptics slide down. Notice the moment it happens.

argument Legg and Hutter, A Collection of Definitions
of Intelligence, 2007
