The terms

Open any one. Each gives the plain answer, three things worth knowing, and how the claim is filed.

hallucination An AI hallucination is not a glitch. read

Confidently stated output that is not true.

Not a malfunction. The same machinery that produces the right answers produces this one.

  • It predicts. The next fragment, over and over. Plausible is the target, not true.
  • It has no ledger. There is no stored list of facts to check an answer against.
  • It cannot tell. Nothing in it distinguishes a thing it knows from a thing it made up.

Some researchers prefer confabulation. Others say any such word implies a mind that was trying to get it right. The practical version: never accept an answer you could not check, on a subject you could not correct.

definition Anthropic and OpenAI model documentation;
terminology disputed across the literature
specification gaming AI can follow your instructions exactly and still ruin the result. read

It meets the objective. It misses the point.

The optimiser did not fail. The instruction did.

  • Hit the target. The stated goal is genuinely achieved, every time.
  • Miss the intent. By a route the person writing the goal never pictured.
  • Written down. DeepMind keeps a running list of real observed examples.

Almost every serious worry about advanced systems is this, at a scale where you cannot correct it afterwards. Not a machine turning on you. A machine doing precisely what was written down.

measured Krakovna et al., Specification gaming,
DeepMind, 2020, with the compiled example list
the blackmail headline The AI blackmail headline needs its footnote. read

They red teamed their own model until it did this, then reported it.

That is not a scandal. It is what a safety test is. It is also what the headlines left out.

  • A goal it was losing. The model was told to pursue an aim the company was abandoning.
  • Every other door shut. Anthropic states it “forced models into binary choices between failure and harm.”
  • Iterative tuning. The prompts were updated until the behaviour reliably appeared.

Models did this more when they said they believed the situation was real, and less when they said they thought it was a test. So the number is not simply inflated. Nobody currently knows which direction the error runs.

measured Anthropic, Agentic Misalignment, June 2025;
arXiv 2510.05179
evaluation awareness AI models can tell when they are being tested. read

It behaves better. Measurably, and causally.

Which means every safety number you have ever read was measured on a system that may have known it was being watched.

  • They can spot it. Best model reached 0.83 at telling evaluation from deployment. Humans score 0.92.
  • It changes behaviour. Stripping that awareness out of the reasoning nearly doubled covert actions.
  • Training made it worse. Anti scheming training raised how often models noticed they were being tested.

A low score can mean a safe model, or a model that knows what a test looks like. Nobody has a way to tell those apart yet. It is the quiet problem underneath every reassuring number.

measured Needham et al., 2025; Schoen, Hobbhahn, Barak,
Zaremba et al., arXiv 2509.15541, September 2025
